Research date: 2026-11-06. ## Q1. Major fulfillment and payment trends with numbers (BNPL share, wallet share, return rates)

### Takeaway
Digital wallets are now the majority e-commerce payment method globally (53% of e-com value in 2024 per Worldpay GPR 2025), BNPL is a steady ~$340B global niche growing toward ~$580B by 2030, and US online return rates sit near 19% (vs ~16% overall). Fulfillment is shifting toward marketplace-run fulfillment (FBA, Fulfilled by Shopee) whose fees keep rising, while in Vietnam COD is fading in favour of e-wallets and VietQR bank transfers.

### Cited Findings
**Payments – global**
- Worldpay Global Payments Report 2025 (10th edition, March 2025): digital wallets = 53% of global e-commerce spend and 32% of POS spend in 2024 — [Worldpay press release](https://www.worldpay.com/en/press-releases/10-years-cash-cards-and-crypto-worldpays-global-payments-report); [NCFA summary](https://ncfacanada.org/gpr-2025-shows-rise-of-wallets-bnpl-and-real-time-payments/)
- Same report: "digital payments" (wallets + A2A + BNPL + crypto) grew from 34% of e-commerce value (2014) to 66% (2024); wallets forecast to reach ~79% of global e-com value by 2030 (commentary citing the report) — [Worldpay](https://www.worldpay.com/en/press-releases/10-years-cash-cards-and-crypto-worldpays-global-payments-report); [dplnews](https://dplnews.com/?p=268924)
- BNPL global e-commerce value: ~$342B in 2024 (from $2.3B in 2015), forecast ~$580B by 2030 — [Worldpay GPR 2025 via NCFA](https://ncfacanada.org/gpr-2025-shows-rise-of-wallets-bnpl-and-real-time-payments/). Conflict: a secondary aggregator (searchlab.nl) quotes wallets at 31% and BNPL at ~5% of global e-com; not reliable vs. Worldpay primary — [searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/en/statistics/online-payments-statistics-2026)

OpenAI ChatGPT "Instant Checkout" runs on the Agentic Commerce Protocol co-developed with Stripe (single-item at launch); Walmart products became purchasable — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/google-brings-checkout-ai-mode-races-rivals-agentic-commerce); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/google-adds-agentic-ai-checkout-to-shopping-140000930.html); [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/google-rolls-out-ucp-powered-checkout-inside-ai-mode-and-gemini)

### Inferences
- Wallet-first, mobile-first checkout is the default design target; BNPL is a conversion lever for higher-AOV categories (~8% of US holiday online spend by Adobe's numbers: $20B/$257.8B), not a mass channel.
- Rising marketplace fulfillment fees (FBA surcharges) push mid-size sellers toward hybrid models: marketplace fulfillment for fast movers, 3PL/own warehouse for long-tail and multichannel orders.
- In Vietnam, low video-commerce AOV ($5–7) means fulfillment/returns cost per order is a large share of margin; COD → VietQR migration reduces failed-delivery/return risk.
- Return rate (~19% online) and return fraud (~9% of returns) make returns analytics a direct P&L lever.

## Q2. How are customer acquisition costs and ad channels shifting (retail media size, AI search impact on traffic)?

### Takeaway
Paid social/search acquisition keeps getting more expensive (Meta CPMs up roughly 13–20% YoY in vendor benchmarks), budgets are moving into retail media (US ~$60B in 2025 → ~$70B+ in 2026, Amazon+Walmart taking ~88–89%) and creators ($37B US 2025), while AI Overviews cut organic CTR by roughly 35–58% for top results even as AI-assistant referral traffic to retailers grows from a small base (+693% holiday 2025).

- For Vietnam sellers, the marketing stack is effectively: marketplace ads (Shopee Ads/TikTok Shop GMV Max-type) + affiliate KOC/livestream + Zalo OA/ZNS for retention; Google SEO matters less than platform search.

## Q3. Reference data architecture for e-commerce analytics (Spark + object storage + table formats) and use cases with measurable ROI

### Takeaway
The consensus open lakehouse pattern is: operational DB CDC + clickstream via Kafka → Spark (batch/Structured Streaming) → Apache Iceberg (or Delta) tables on S3-compatible object storage, organised as bronze/silver/gold, with a catalog (Nessie/REST/Hive/Unity/Glue) and Trino/Spark/Flink as engines. Grab migrated a petabyte-scale Hive-Parquet lake on S3 to Iceberg. MinIO community edition entered maintenance mode in Dec 2025, so new self-hosted builds should evaluate alternatives (SeaweedFS, Ceph/Rook, Garage, RustFS) or a cloud S3. Demand forecasting and personalization/recommendations are the most commonly cited ROI use cases, but most ROI numbers are vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
**Architecture patterns**
- Reference local stack (Manning Iceberg lakehouse book): PostgreSQL as operational source, Spark as ingestion engine, MinIO as S3-compatible storage, Nessie as Iceberg catalog, Docker Compose — [Manning](https://livebook.manning.com/learn/concepts/spark--platform/architecting-an-apache-iceberg-lakehouse)
- MinIO's Iceberg guidance: Iceberg on MinIO with multi-engine access (Spark, Flink, Trino) — [MinIO blog](https://blog.min.io/lakehouse-architecture-iceberg-minio); [MinIO blog 2](https://blog.min.io/building-a-data-lakehouse-using-apache-iceberg-and-minio/)
- Retail case (vendor Ksolves): Kafka → Spark Structured Streaming 4.1.1 → Iceberg 1.10.1 on MinIO, queried by Trino, for multi-store retail — [Ksolves](https://www.ksolves.com/case-studies/big-data/apache-iceberg-nifi-spark-streaming-retail-lakehouse-global-stores)
- Clickstream design notes: handle out-of-order and duplicate events; micro-batch preferred over pure streaming for operational simplicity; compact small files; build aggregate tables (e.g., fact_funnel_daily) rather than rescanning raw events — [Substack design walkthrough](https://ssagi.substack.com/p/data-engineering-system-design-clickstream)
- Grab (SEA super-app with food/mart commerce): migrated petabyte-scale data lake on S3 from Hive Parquet to Iceberg; issues included Hive lock contention, Spark 3.4/Iceberg timestamp compatibility, S3 storage-tier cost spikes during migration; Iceberg is default format for two internal systems; open-sourced UnifiedSparkCatalog; aims to stay format-agnostic but sees Iceberg as most mature — [Grab Engineering](https://engineering.grab.com/our-journey-to-apache-iceberg-adoption) (via search summary; direct fetch blocked)
- Airbnb (older, 2022-era) moved warehouse to Iceberg + Spark 3 — [Airbnb Engineering](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/upgrading-data-warehouse-infrastructure-at-airbnb-a4e18f09b6d5)
- Another rebuild: legacy HBase + Elasticsearch replaced with Iceberg/Parquet, Spark ingestion, Trino + Doris serving — [DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/how-we-rebuilt-a-legacy-hbase-elasticsearch-system)

**MinIO licensing risk (important for Spark+MinIO designs)**
- May 2025: management console features/LDAP-OIDC removed from community UI; Oct 2025: community Docker images/binaries dropped; early Dec 2025: community edition in maintenance mode (no new features/PRs; security fixes case-by-case); users pushed to paid AIStor — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2025/12/minio-s3-api-alternatives/); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/minio-moves-away-from-open-source/); [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/minio-ends-active-development/)
- Alternatives suggested: Garage, SeaweedFS, RustFS, Ceph via Rook (large clusters), Zenko CloudServer (AGPL); no drop-in fork with traction as of Dec 2025 — [ayedo](https://ayedo.de/en/posts/minio-im-maintenance-mode/); [DEV](https://dev.to/alexandrev/minio-in-maintenance-mode-what-it-means-for-the-community-edition-oem-users-and-open-source-3o4j)

**Attribution after privacy changes**
- Google Meridian open-source Bayesian MMM: introduced Mar 2024, generally available Jan 2025; free on GitHub; integrates with Google's MMM Data Platform; third-party estimate of setup cost €40–50k — [Martech](https://martech.org/google-releases-its-open-source-marketing-mix-model-to-all/); [fifty-five](https://it.fifty-five.com/tea-house/everything-you-need-to-know-about-meridian)

**ROI of use cases (mostly secondary/vendor)**
- Personalization: 10–15% revenue lift (range 5–25%) attributed to McKinsey "Next in Personalization" 2021 (older data, secondhand) — [Envive summary](https://www.envive.ai/post/ai-personalization-in-ecommerce-lift-statistics?)
- Demand forecasting described as fastest-payback AI use case via inventory carrying cost and stockouts; claims of 60–75% stockout reduction / 25–40% lower carrying cost lack primary sourcing — [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/ai-in-retail); [GUVI](https://www.guvi.in/blog/ai-in-retail-demand-forecasting/)
- Return fraud ≈ 9% of US returns (fraud-detection ROI baseline) — [NRF](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025)

### Inferences
Typical reference architecture (synthesised from sources above; not a single published standard):
1. Sources: OLTP (orders, inventory, customers) via CDC (Debezium) → Kafka; web/app clickstream SDK → Kafka; marketplace APIs (Shopee/TikTok Shop/Lazada order & ads reports), ad platforms, payment gateway, ZNS/email logs via batch connectors (Airbyte/custom).
2. Storage: S3 / GCS or self-hosted S3-compatible store (MinIO now risky → SeaweedFS/Ceph/cloud S3); Iceberg (or Delta) tables in bronze (raw) → silver (cleaned, deduped, sessionized) → gold (fact_orders, fact_funnel_daily, dim_customer, customer_360, inventory snapshots).
3. Compute: Spark batch + Structured Streaming (or Flink for low-latency); orchestration Airflow/Dagster; dbt on Spark/Trino for gold models; table maintenance (compaction, snapshot expiry).
4. Serving: Trino/StarRocks/Doris for BI (Superset/Metabase); feature store (Feast/Redis) for online ML; vector/ANN index for recommendations; reverse-ETL to CDP/ads (audiences, conversion APIs).
5. ML use cases: demand forecasting (per-SKU daily, LightGBM/Prophet-type), recommendations (candidate generation + ranking, real-time features from stream), dynamic/competitive pricing, fraud & return-abuse scoring (COD-refusal risk in Vietnam), RFM/CLV churn segmentation driving ZNS/email, MMM (Meridian/Robyn) + incrementality tests for attribution.

Small-to-mid seller vs large platform:
- Small/mid seller (Shopify/marketplace-based, <~1M orders/yr): a full Spark lakehouse is usually overkill; realistic stack = managed ELT into BigQuery/Postgres/DuckDB-on-object-storage, dbt, a BI tool, marketplace analytics, a lightweight CDP/email-ZNS tool, spreadsheet/Prophet forecasting, and MMM-lite or platform-reported attribution. Spark + Iceberg on object storage becomes justified with high-volume clickstream, multi-marketplace consolidation, or data-sovereignty/cost reasons for self-hosting.
- Large platform (Shopee/Grab/Tiki-scale): petabyte lakehouse on object storage with Iceberg, multiple engines (Spark/Flink/Trino), streaming feature pipelines, in-house recommendation/ranking, fraud and pricing systems, retail-media ad stack with clean-room style measurement — consistent with Grab's Iceberg migration.
- Highest measurable-ROI first projects for mid-size sellers: (1) demand forecasting/replenishment (inventory and stockout costs), (2) RFM/CLV segmentation + triggered messaging (cheap channels, direct revenue attribution), (3) return/COD-risk scoring, (4) MMM to reallocate rising paid spend.
